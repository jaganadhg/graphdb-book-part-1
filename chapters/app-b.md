# Appendix B: Facilitation and delivery guide

## Suggested schedule

The program runs comfortably across four working sessions:

| Session | Part | Duration | Format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Part II | 30–45 min | Guided setup and validation |
| 2 | Part III | 60 min | Modeling, schema, and import |
| 3 | Part IV | 60 min | Lineage traversal workshop |
| 4 | Part V | 60–75 min | Impact analysis and capstone |

Part I is reading, not a session; circulate it beforehand, and walk leadership through it separately if they are the intended audience. Parts VI and VII are best treated as follow-on material: schedule Part VI as a 30-minute glossary review after the capstone, and Part VII as a short demo once everyone has Docker or Podman installed.

## Teaching posture

- Keep the graph small enough to reason about manually.
- Emphasize semantic clarity over feature breadth.
- Never present graph as a universal replacement for relational systems: return to the decision rule whenever someone over-reaches.
- Require validation checks after every load or modeling step.
- Ask learners to explain what each edge *means* in operational terms.
- For teams that learn by discussion, reserve the final 15 minutes of each session for model critique. This reinforces the central idea: graph design is an engineering judgment, not a syntax exercise.
